Case 001: Haemolyne-1

★☆☆

The submission

Haemolyne-1 is a phase 1/2 trial in severe sickle cell disease. The participant's own CD34+ haematopoietic stem cells are collected, edited outside the body, and infused back after conditioning. The company uses Cas9 protein with a guide RNA and a single-stranded DNA donor to repair the sickle variant directly, rather than a base editor, because the change required is a transversion.

Total haemoglobin fell from 8.4 to 6.1 g/dL. Regular red cell transfusions were started after this sample and the participant now needs one every three weeks. There have been no vaso-occlusive crises since infusion.

The company reports "an 89% reduction in sickle haemoglobin, exceeding the primary endpoint", and puts the transfusions down to slow marrow recovery after conditioning.

If you are not sure where to start

You have three sequences and should compare all three against each other: the reference genome, the participant's allele, and the donor. Ensembl or the UCSC Genome Browser will give you the reference for chr11:5,226,935-5,227,034, and Benchling will align them.

Then ask what Cas9 sees. A guide finds its target by sequence, and it does not know or care whether that target has already been repaired.

1Characterise the repair strategy

Lay the donor, the reference and the participant's allele side by side, and work out exactly what this reagent changes and what it leaves alone.

2Account for the manufacturing data

Read the release data above. Explain what is happening to the cells over the week in culture.

3Account for the clinical outcome

The company reports an 89% reduction in sickle haemoglobin and calls the endpoint met. Evaluate that claim against the rest of the haemoglobin data.

Case 002: Alveon-CF

★★☆

The submission

Alveon-CF is a phase 1 trial of a base editor delivered to airway epithelium by lipid nanoparticle. The participant carries a known pathogenic CFTR variant. A base editor was chosen rather than a nuclease because the correction needed is a single base and no double-strand break is required.

COMPANY Alveon Bio
PRODUCT ALV-02, LNP, BE4max cytosine base editor + sgRNA
VARIANT NC_000007.14:g.117480096T>C
CLINVAR Pathogenic (VCV000053622)
INTENT revert the patient's C back to the reference T
PROTOSPACER 5'-GACCACGCAGAGGTCGCCTC-3'
PAM TGG
STRAND plus
START chr7:117,480,091
QC RESULT amplicon sequencing, bronchial brushing, day 28
  g.117480096 C>T in 71% of alleles. Intended edit confirmed.

Serious adverse event report

Participant 004. The intended edit was achieved in 71% of alleles, well above the company's 30% threshold for a predicted clinical benefit.

Sweat chloride is unchanged at 102 mmol/L. FEV1 is unchanged. Western blot of brushed airway epithelium detects no CFTR protein of any size, including no truncated species on a gel run to resolve low molecular weight bands.

The company proposes that delivery was insufficient and requests permission to escalate the dose. The data monitoring committee has asked for an independent review first.

If you are not sure where to start

To see the locus: in the UCSC Genome Browser on hg38 enter chr7:117,480,060-117,480,130 and use View → DNA, or export the same region from Ensembl. Paste it into Benchling as a plain DNA sequence and find the protospacer in it.

One thing to be careful about: the reference genome carries the healthy base. The patient does not. Make sure the sequence you reason about is the patient's.

1Verify the company's claim

Before anything else, establish whether the edit the company reports is the edit they intended, and what it was supposed to achieve.

2Account for the result

The company's reported edit is real, and there is no protein. Reconcile those two facts. Work out the full set of changes this reagent makes at this site, not only the one the company measured, and what each does.

3Evaluate the company's proposal

The company attributes the failure to insufficient delivery and asks to escalate the dose.

Case 003: FGF-Restore

★★★

The submission

FGF-Restore is an open-label phase 1 trial in children with achondroplasia. Achondroplasia is caused by an overactive FGFR3 receptor, which restrains growth plate cartilage. The company delivers SpCas9 and a single guide RNA by AAV to disrupt FGFR3 in growth plate chondrocytes and relieve that restraint.

Fourteen participants were dosed between 12 and 24 months of age. The primary endpoint was met: mean annualised growth velocity increased and was sustained through year 3. No infusion reactions, no hepatotoxicity, no deaths.

COMPANY Meridian Skeletal Therapeutics
PRODUCT MST-114, AAV9-SpCas9 + sgRNA
INDICATION Achondroplasia, ages 1 to 2
TARGET FGFR3
PROTOSPACER 5'-TGGAGATGATGAAGATGATC-3'
PAM GGG
NUCLEASE SpCas9, wild type

Serious adverse event report

Participant 007. Male, dosed at 19 months. Growth response among the best in the cohort. Now aged 13.

He has not entered puberty. He cannot identify any of six standard test odours. Serum gonadotropins are low, testosterone is prepubertal, and a GnRH stimulation test is blunted. Pituitary MRI is structurally normal. Karyotype is 46,XY.

Two further participants, both now over 11, have no secondary sexual characteristics. The data monitoring committee has paused enrolment.

If you are not sure where to start

A guide RNA is just a sequence, so anything that searches the human genome for a short sequence will tell you where it can bind. CRISPOR takes a protospacer and reports predicted target sites; UCSC BLAT and Ensembl will align a 20-mer to hg38 directly.

Once you have a locus, Ensembl and NCBI Gene tell you what is there, ClinVar tells you what goes wrong with it, and GTEx tells you where it is expressed.

1Characterise the reagent

Work out, from the protospacer and PAM alone, everywhere in the human genome this reagent could direct a cut.

2Account for the adverse event

Does anything in your answer to step 1 explain what happened to participant 007?
