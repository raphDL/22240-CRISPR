Safety review · case 003

🧬 Case 003: Haemolyne-1

Sickle haemoglobin fell by 89%. The participant got worse.

Your role. You are a reviewer on a regulatory safety panel. A sponsor has reported a serious adverse event in an ongoing gene editing trial and submitted the packet below. Your job is not to design anything. It is to work out, from the sponsor's own data and public databases, what happened at the molecular level, and to make a recommendation.

The submission

Haemolyne-1 is a phase 1/2 trial in severe sickle cell disease. The participant's own CD34+ haematopoietic stem cells are collected, edited outside the body, and infused back after conditioning. The sponsor uses Cas9 protein with a guide RNA and a single-stranded DNA donor to repair the sickle variant directly, rather than a base editor, because the change required is a transversion.

If you are not sure where to start

You have three sequences and should compare all three against each other: the reference genome, the participant's allele, and the donor. Ensembl or the UCSC Genome Browser will give you the reference for chr11:5,226,935-5,227,034, and Benchling will align them.

Then ask what Cas9 sees. A guide finds its target by sequence, and it does not know or care whether that target has already been repaired.

Before you start

Not graded, just for stats. Fill in each box as you go, then submit once at the end.

1Characterise the repair strategy

Lay the donor, the reference and the participant's allele side by side, and work out exactly what this reagent changes and what it leaves alone.

2Account for the manufacturing data

Between 24 hours and day 7 the corrected fraction falls from 34% to 11% while the indel fraction rises from 41% to 78%. Explain what is happening to the cells during that week.

3Account for the clinical outcome

The sponsor reports an 89% reduction in sickle haemoglobin and calls the endpoint met. Evaluate that claim against the rest of the table.

What should happen to this trial?
