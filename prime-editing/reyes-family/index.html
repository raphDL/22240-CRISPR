🧬 Cure the Reyes Family

Find the deletion. Design the fix.

The case

A newborn in the Reyes family was diagnosed with severe, persistent high blood sugar in the first weeks of life, well outside the usual range for a healthy infant. No one else in the family has diabetes. Genetic testing was ordered to look for a cause.

The clinical genetics lab sequenced the full genomic locus of one candidate gene from the infant's blood. That's the file below. No gene name attached, that's step 1.

Your job: figure out what's broken, decide whether it's fixable, and design the edit that would do it.

⬇ Download patient_variant.fasta
One FASTA record, ~1.7 kb of genomic sequence.

Tools

You'll use Benchling and BLAST for this exercise.

Before you start

Not graded, just for stats. Fill in each box as you go, then submit once at the end.

1Which gene is this?

BLAST the FASTA against the human genome or RefSeq mRNA database to find the top hit. Keep each answer to a few words.

2Bring it into Benchling

Get the gene's reference genomic sequence, not just the mRNA or CDS, and bring it into Benchling.

If it doesn't work, click here

The most reliable route: on NCBI's Nucleotide database, search <gene symbol>[gene] AND Homo sapiens[orgn] AND refseqgene[filter]. That filters straight to the genomic RefSeqGene record (introns included) and skips picking a transcript entirely. Open the result whose title matches your gene, not a nearby overlapping gene, then Send to → File → GenBank (full) to download a .gb file. Drag that straight into Benchling. Benchling's own Import from Database can be flaky, this skips it entirely.

3Find the variant

Align the patient sequence to the gene's reference genomic sequence. This time the patient's sequence is shorter than the reference. Find where, and how much is missing.

Translate the spliced coding sequence (mind the introns) on both sides to work out what's missing at the protein level.

4Choose your editor

A base editor converts one base to another, it can't add or remove nucleotides. Given what you found in Step 3, which class of editor does this variant actually need?

5Design the pegRNA

A prime editor pairs a Cas9 nickase with a reverse transcriptase, guided by a pegRNA. The spacer and PAM direct the nick, same idea as a normal gRNA. The pegRNA's 3' extension does the rest: the primer binding site (PBS) anneals to the nicked strand's exposed 3' end and primes synthesis; the RT template (RTT) is what the reverse transcriptase copies to write the new sequence.

Find a spacer and PAM near the deletion, on either strand, ideally with the nick close to the missing bases. Then work out what the PBS and RTT need to encode to put those bases back.

Show the before and after sequence, marking every base that changes. Example: TCCCTGC_GCCCCC → TCCCTGC*A*GCCCCC (a base put back where one was missing)
