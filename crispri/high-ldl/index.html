🧬 Patient with high LDL

Same gene. No edits. Less protein.

The case

A 48-year-old has LDL cholesterol that stays dangerously high on the maximum tolerated dose of a statin. Hepatocytes clear LDL from the blood using LDL receptors on their surface. A liver biopsy was sequenced by RNA-seq, and one transcript stands out as far more abundant than in matched controls.

The team wants to test whether turning that transcript down helps this patient. Because it's a first-in-patient study, they want an intervention they can stop. Nothing permanent, and nothing that changes the genome.

Your job: nothing here is mutated. Work out which tool fits the brief, then design where to aim it.

3Design your guides

Your contig is a transcript, so it has no promoter in it. Pull the gene's reference genomic sequence into Benchling and align the contig to it. Where the contig's first base lands is the transcription start site (TSS), the first base that gets transcribed. It is not the start codon.

Take the region from 1,000 bp upstream to 300 bp downstream of the TSS (1,301 bp total) and paste it below.

If Benchling's import doesn't work, click here

On NCBI's Nucleotide database, search <gene symbol>[gene] AND Homo sapiens[orgn] AND refseqgene[filter]. That filters straight to the genomic RefSeqGene record (introns and flanking sequence included) and skips picking a transcript entirely. Open the result whose title matches your gene, not a nearby overlapping gene, then Send to → File → GenBank (full) to download a .gb file and drag that into Benchling.

Every SpCas9 protospacer (20 nt + NGG) in your window, on both strands, ranked. Offset is the distance from the TSS to the middle of the protospacer, negative upstream, positive downstream.

The score is a simplified teaching model built for this exercise: position relative to the TSS, GC content, and a penalty for a TTTT run (which terminates Pol III transcription of the guide). It is not a published CRISPRi predictor, and it knows nothing about chromatin.
