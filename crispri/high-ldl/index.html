🧬 Patient with high LDL

The case

A 48-year-old has LDL cholesterol that stays dangerously high on the maximum tolerated dose of a statin. Hepatocytes clear LDL from the blood using LDL receptors on their surface. A liver biopsy was sequenced by RNA-seq, and one transcript stands out as far more abundant than in matched controls.

The team wants to test whether turning that transcript down helps this patient. Because it's a first-in-patient study, they want an intervention they can stop. Nothing permanent, and nothing that changes the genome.

Your job: nothing here is mutated. Work out which tool fits the brief, then design where to aim it.

3Design your guides

Your contig is a transcript, so it has no promoter in it. Pull the gene's reference genomic sequence into Benchling and align the contig to it. Where the contig's first base lands is the transcription start site (TSS), the first base that gets transcribed. It is not the start codon, and it is not where the gene annotation begins either (see the hint below for why).

How to get the genomic sequence into Benchling

Click the + in the top left → DNA/RNA sequence → Import DNA/RNA sequence → Import from database. Type your gene symbol under Sequence, put GRCh38 under Genome, and hit Search. Pick the transcript you want, set Import as: Genomic sequence, and set Upstream bases to 1000 so you get the promoter as well as the gene.

One trap. The imported sequence has both a gene annotation and an Exon 1 annotation, and they do not start in the same place. The gene box spans every annotated isoform at once, so it begins wherever the earliest one begins, which is usually upstream of the transcript you actually imported. It is not a TSS. Genes don't have one TSS, transcripts do. Use the Exon 1 annotation of your transcript, and confirm it against where your contig aligns: the contig's first base is the TSS by definition.
